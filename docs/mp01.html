#' Acquire Netflix What We Watched Reports
#' 
#' This function will acquire and standardize all data for MP#01
#' from Netflix. Because Netflix hosts the data files behind a CDN, we
#' are accessing the release pages and automatically extracting links to the
#' relevant files (with CDN hash). 
#' 
#' After import, files are stacked vertically and some basic cleaning is
#' performed. dim
load_netflix_data <- function(){
  # Note to students: Make sure you have installed the tidyverse and fs packages
  #                   before beginning this assignment. If you aren't sure,
  #                   simply run `library(tidyverse)` at your R Console and, if
  #                   you don't see an error message, `tidyverse` has been 
  #                   installed. (Same for `fs`) If you still need to install
  #                   either package, use the `install.packages` function as 
  #                   demonstrated in class.
  library(tidyverse)
  library(rvest)
  library(readxl)
  library(fs)
  
  data_dir <- path("data", "mp01")
    
  if(!dir_exists(data_dir)){
    dir_create(data_dir, showWarnings=FALSE, recurse=TRUE)
  }
  
  NETFLIX_URLS <- list(
    `2023H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-a-netflix-engagement-report",
    `2023H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2023",
    `2024H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2024",
    `2024H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2024",
    `2025H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2025",
    `2025H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2025",
    `2026H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2026"
  )
  
  download_if_needed <- function(url, local){
    if(!file_exists(local)){
      download.file(url, local, quiet = TRUE, mode = "wb")
    }
    
    return(local)
  }
    
  NETFLIX_URLS |> 
    map(read_html) |> 
    map(html_element, xpath = "//a[contains(@href, '.xlsx')]") |> 
    map_chr(html_attr, "href") |> 
    enframe("period", "url") |> 
   mutate(path = path(data_dir, paste0(period, ".xlsx")), 
          path = map2(url, path, download_if_needed), 
          data = map(path, read_excel, skip=3, na="*"), 
          data = map(data, \(x) x |> mutate(`Release Date` = as.Date(`Release Date`)))) |>
    unnest(data) |> 
    select(-url, -path) |> 
    mutate(year=str_sub(period, 1, 4), 
           half=str_sub(period, start=6), 
           period_start = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 1, 7), 1), 
           period_end = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 7, 12), if_else(half==1, 30, 31))) |> 
    filter(!is.na(`Hours Viewed`)) |> 
    mutate(live_event = !is.na(Runtime) & str_detect(Runtime, "\\d+:\\d+\\*"), 
           clean_time = str_remove(Runtime, fixed("*")),
           Runtime = 60 * hour(hm(clean_time)) + minute(hm(clean_time))) |>
    select(-clean_time) |>
    mutate(Runtime = coalesce(Runtime, mean(Runtime, na.rm=TRUE)), 
           .by=Title, 
           Title = stringi::stri_trans_general(Title, "Latin-ASCII"),
           Title = str_remove(Title, " // .*"), 
           Title = str_remove(Title, "[^[:ascii:]].*"), 
           Title = str_trim(Title)) |>
    filter_out(Title == "") |>
    select(year, 
           period, 
           period_start, 
           period_end, 
           Title, 
           -half, 
           Runtime, 
           Views, 
           `Hours Viewed`, 
           `Release Date`,
           `Available Globally?`, 
           live_event)
}

Task 7: Corporate Strategy Memo

To: Netflix Leadership
From: Hannatu Shehu
Subject: Original Content Strategy for 2027 and Beyond

Based on my analysis of Netflix viewing data, my core recommendation is that the company focus its 2027 original content strategy on three areas; television shows claim a much larger share of viewing time than movies, globally available titles punch well above their weight compared to their share of the catalog, our Top 10 blockbusters account for a relatively small slice of overall viewing, showing that the wider catalog is essential for keeping subscribers engaged. My analysis of viewing time states that Shows account for 69.79% of total viewing time in the first half of 2026, whereas movies represent 28.73% with the remaining 1.48% unclassified. The audience’s attention is heavily focused on series; we should dedicate a larger share of our original content investment to series production. But that doesn’t mean we should drop movies entirely; it just means the data supports greater emphasis on series rather than a lack of movies. The smart decision for the future is to invest more in series that can sustain multiple seasons and develop long-term franchises.

Geographic reach is another recommendation for content performance. Globally available titles make up 35.09% of our catalog, and they drive 51.92% of total viewing hours. In other words, globally available titles represent less than half of the catalog but account for more than half of viewing. This shows that content that can reach audiences across several markets could be very valuable; we should prioritize originals with strong international distribution potential. This could allow successful local productions to reach larger international audiences.

At the same time, we shouldn’t only focus on blockbusters, our broader catalog does the heavy lifting day to day. In the first half of 2026, our Top 10 titles accounted for only 6.70% of total watch time. That means roughly 93.30% of viewing happened outside those ten titles. We shouldn’t judge our overall content health solely by blockbuster releases. Although these releases can attract attention and strengthen the content health, these results show the importance of the broader catalog. Viewers have different interests; Netflix serves different audiences. Therefore, the company should balance spending on major releases with continued investment in smaller and more diverse original productions.

In conclusion, our 2027 strategy should focus on three recommendations: prioritizing original series, increasing investment in globally available titles, and maintaining a broad catalog. By combining all three strategies, Netflix can build a content strategy that reflects how viewers use the platform.
